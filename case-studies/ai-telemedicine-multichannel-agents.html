The Problem: Answering at National Scale

For a high-volume telemedicine practice, the same questions arrive all day across three channels at once — phone, website chat, and SMS. Prospective patients want to know what a programme costs, whether they are eligible, and what is actually included. None of it is clinical, but answering it manually pulls licensed staff away from patient care, and the volume only grows as the ad spend does.

A virtual telemedicine and wellness practice, with licensed providers in all 50 US states and a high-volume ad funnel driving leads in daily, faced exactly this. Its existing tooling was fragmented rather than absent: a generic third-party chatbot answered on the website in the wrong brand voice and with information it did not reliably have, SMS went out as one-way blasts with no handling of the replies they generated, and there was no voice automation at all — leaving the busiest channel the least supported.

The Solution: A Multi-Channel AI Agent — Four Agents, One Ava

GrowwStacks built four AI agents — all answering as one assistant named Ava — sharing a single knowledge base and a single CRM logging system. Each was purpose-built for the constraints of its own channel rather than one script copy-pasted across four surfaces: an inbound voice agent, an outbound voice agent, a website chat agent, and an SMS agent. Voice needs pacing and interruption handling, SMS needs brevity and segment discipline, and chat needs formatting and clickable links.

The result is the same accurate, on-brand, compliant experience whether a lead calls the main line, replies to a text, or opens the chat widget. Every conversation is logged to GoHighLevel automatically — a contact record created or updated, plus a readable summary of what was discussed — with no manual data entry from staff on any channel.

How It Works — One Assistant Across Four Channels

Each agent handles the constraints of its own channel while sharing the same facts, the same boundaries, and the same logging. Here is what each one does:

  1. Inbound Voice: Answers the main line as Ava and handles general questions from the shared knowledge base. When a question needs specifics it does not already have, it queries a knowledge-base tool on demand — speaking a short "one moment" filler while it does, so there is no dead air. It captures and confirms contact details, and escalates live on clear triggers: a medical emergency routes to 911, and requests for individualised advice, frustration, complaints, and billing disputes all go to a person.
  2. Outbound Voice: Fires automatically when a new tagged lead enters the CRM, and opens on that lead's actual stated interest pulled from their enquiry form rather than a generic greeting. It qualifies naturally instead of reading a checklist, then logs a structured outcome — interested and awaiting scheduling, wants more info, declined, callback requested, or voicemail. It never promises to book or send something it can't deliver; it offers a human follow-up instead.
  3. Website Chat: Answers the visitor's first question with no gate at all. Only from the second question does it ask for details, framed naturally as part of the conversation rather than as a form. The widget was rebuilt from scratch to match the client's site, with reliable, clickable, program-specific links — and every conversation logs a structured summary to the CRM.
  4. SMS: Knows the lead's number instantly, looks them up in the CRM, and never re-asks for what it already has. Formatting is plain text only, capped to roughly two segments, since Markdown renders literally on a phone and long messages fragment across carriers. It also solves the single-destination Twilio routing constraint by handling messages itself, while logging every inbound and outbound message to the contact record.
The SMS agent end to end — an inbound webhook hands the message to the AI agent, which runs on a chat model with conversation memory and a set of tools it can call.
One agent, many tools — the AI agent connects to the shared knowledge base and CRM actions (create contact, update notes, send message, search contact) through a single reusable pattern.

💡 One brain, four voices — Because all four agents read from the same knowledge base and share the same logging pattern, updating a price or an eligibility rule once propagates to voice, chat, and SMS at the same time. No re-training, no drift between channels.

Before vs. After

Before: A generic third-party chatbot answered website visitors in the wrong brand voice, SMS went out as one-way blasts with no reply handling, and there was no voice automation at all. Leads reached the CRM inconsistently, and staff spent hours each day answering the same questions about pricing and eligibility.

After: A fully custom, on-brand AI system runs across four channels. Every touchpoint logs itself automatically to GoHighLevel, outbound voice and SMS personalise on the lead's stated interest, and content updates reach every channel at once because the knowledge base is shared — with zero manual data entry.

Implementation: Live in 6 Weeks

  1. Knowledge Base First: Built one shared knowledge-base document as the single source of truth for pricing, eligibility, and programme details across all four agents.
  2. Reusable CRM Pattern: Built a single lookup → create/update → log pattern once and reused it on every channel, cutting build time on each successive agent.
  3. Voice Agents: Configured inbound and outbound voice on VAPI and Twilio, with escalation triggers and on-demand knowledge-base lookups.
  4. Chat & SMS Agents: Built the chat and SMS agents on n8n behind webhooks, with a custom-branded chat widget and SMS-specific plain-text formatting.
  5. Prompt Engineering & Compliance: Tuned tool-call sequencing, static non-guessable system parameters for IDs and phone numbers, channel-specific formatting, and identical compliance guardrails across all four agents.

The Right Fit — and When It Isn't

This suits high-volume practices and service businesses fielding the same questions across phone, chat, and text — where answers must stay consistent and compliant, and every lead needs to reach the CRM. The more channels a business runs and the more regulated its answers, the more a shared knowledge base pays for itself. It pairs naturally with an AI voice receptionist and booking system on the inbound side, and applies the same CRM-first discipline as a GoHighLevel-centred referral and recruiting engine or an AI WhatsApp support bot that logs every conversation automatically.

It is less suited to low-volume operations where one person comfortably handles everything inbound, since it adds architecture without solving a real problem. It also fits poorly where answers are so bespoke per customer that no shared knowledge base can capture them — if every enquiry needs individual judgement, there is nothing consistent to share.
